# Reviewed component comparison

Open the Compare page, or use `http://127.0.0.1:8765/?page=compare` directly. The first card now offers an unsaved structured workflow: select a searchable catalog item by exact name/suffix and mod level on each side, or choose an existing saved planned build. The slot filter follows Helmet, Mask, Gloves, Shoes, Chest/Top, Legs/Bottoms, then weapon and armor choices. Keyboard Enter selects the first visible search result; the result list also supports native arrow-key selection and Enter. Escape clears one side. Clear comparison resets both sides and optional context. Selections and results survive switching away from Compare and back during the page session, but do not write to inventory, ownership, Field Guide, or saved builds.

Optional structured context fields accept HP and scenario, baseline damage pools, two-component old damage share, RPM/magazine/reload, low-HP uptime and strength, and an explicit direct-hit encounter with crit/weakspot overlap and duration. Missing timing or hit inputs produce a visible request for the required fields; blanks are not filled from Maurice's personal build. Separate community set and derived suffix checkboxes opt into assumption scenarios; both default off and never establish a game winner. This policy applies to selected catalog items and saved plans. Incomplete profiles appear prominently beside result percentages; unknown attributes are never silently interpreted as absent effects. The original JSON editor remains in the collapsed Reviewed component comparison card for advanced inputs and measured runs. `POST /api/component-compare-structured` resolves the user's selections server-side and returns the same component evidence, with selection provenance and saved-plan limitations.

The Compare page includes an unsaved **Reviewed component comparison** panel. It accepts two item or build descriptions, optional shared context, and separate measured runs. An item with an exact `catalog_id` and `level` automatically loads its catalog numeric rows when `attributes` is omitted; supplied rows remain explicit overrides. The result carries source status, frame hash, recording context and observed level for reviewed rows. It does not require an owned inventory item. The same evaluator also annotates the existing same-slot catalog comparison and the saved planned-build comparison. The saved-build path reads every selected gear and mod row, retaining incomplete numeric profiles as explicit gaps.

An alternative can contain `attributes` directly or an `items` array of gear and mods. Each row names its damage or timing attribute, numeric percentage, optional condition and source. A row with `conditional_bonus_percent` keeps its base value even when the additional condition is inactive. For a wholly conditional value, put the condition on the row without `conditional_bonus_percent`. `condition_uptime` in context accepts the fraction of time active from 0 to 1 keyed by condition text. A scaling `low HP` row also needs a separate `condition_strength` fraction of its listed maximum; entering uptime alone cannot assert the maximum magnitude. `hp_percent` resolves only explicit thresholds such as `below 30%`; a vague low-HP curve remains unresolved even at a specified HP. User-entered rows with unverified or missing source status are labeled as input assumptions in the result.

Supported listed pools are Weapon, Crit, Weakspot, Elemental, Status, Power Surge, Shock, Unstable Bomber, All Damage, Fire Rate, Magazine Capacity and Reload Efficiency. The evaluator calculates normal, crit, weakspot, crit plus weakspot, generic status, elemental non-status, Power Surge, Shock and isolated Unstable Bomber relative damage branches. The Bomber branch applies only its named bonus and All Damage; other bucket applicability remains unverified. Optional `old_damage_shares` supplies a conditional weighted comparison; shares must sum to one. Optional `timing` with RPM, magazine and reload seconds yields floor/ceil magazine long-run shot-throughput sensitivity. Unknown conditions shared by both alternatives are evaluated in the same endpoint state, so identical conditional builds cancel exactly. The engine never treats this as a measured sustained DPS result.

Eight exact-level current Field Guide main effects have sourced arithmetic. The first three are Distant Strike <Survival> 10 (weakspot bonus bounded by distance), Against All Odds <Precision> 7 (base Weapon/Status plus bounded HP-consumption stacks), and Power of Striving <Downstar Deviant Energy> 9 (base Weapon/Status; conditional magnitude remains unknown). A sourced Treacherous Tides two-piece rule contributes Weapon/Status under entered PvE non-RaidZone and HP conditions. Its three-piece rule contributes the released +8% endpoint at 100% HP and +28% at or below 60% HP; intermediate HP retains an 8–28% range because the curve is unpublished. Stacking with two-piece and other pools remains a stated sensitivity assumption. The four-piece 30% shield and eight-second cooldown are preview test values, not certified current numbers. These rules also enter saved planned-build comparisons when the exact items and set count are selected. Main-effect text at other levels is not silently promoted to these values.

Five more exact-level cards were visually checked in the held October Field Guide recording and passed source-image hash validation: Static Shock <Survival> 11 (+20% Power Surge packet damage, with the -50% status duration retained as a missing timing effect); Surge Amplifier <Survival> 10 (+5% Power Surge damage per active stack, up to four, three seconds); Overflow <General> 8 (+30% magazine after an empty reload); Bullet Siphon <Aero Deviant Energy> 12 (+5% Weapon damage plus 4% per five bullets consumed, extra capped at 20%); and Elemental Overload <Resonance> 9 (+18% to the four listed elements). Their operators accept explicit stack, consumed-bullet, and empty-reload state inputs and keep unresolved event timing visible. These exact readings do not authorize transferring magnitudes to other levels or variants.

Two verified Shiny Lv. 17 cards have separate conditional operators when the alternative explicitly sets `shiny: true`: Ferocious Charge adds up to 20% Weapon/Status after a qualifying nearby kill for eight seconds, and Elemental Resonance adds up to 24% Elemental from previous-magazine events for 15 seconds after reload. `condition_uptime` can resolve the kill buff; `prior_magazine_elemental_events` and `seconds_since_reload` can resolve a stated resonance scenario. Ordinary versions do not inherit the Shiny card. Event counting, refresh, and applicability to status remain listed as unverified.

The [official Wind Intepreter Cap rule](https://www.oncehuman.game/news/update/20260709/40780_1307147.html) adds a scoped +25% Unstable Bomber damage rate for non-RaidZone scenarios. The comparison exposes it in a separate keyword branch when the armor is selected and the scenario is entered. Bomber explosion frequency, its ammo return, and how its damage rate stacks with other bonuses remain explicit gaps.

The [October Snowline Elite Vest rule](https://www.oncehuman.game/news/update/20261021/40780_1317280.html) repeats effects triggered by a Frost Vortex once without generating a second Vortex. The packet model can apply that to an event tagged `trigger_family: "frost_vortex"` and `triggered_effect: true`, leaving the Vortex packet unchanged. The comparison requires the explicit `snowline_rule_active: true` scenario input and a tagged packet; it does not infer Ice Spear damage or Frost Vortex frequency from the armor name.

The [January 2026 Bastille conversion](https://www.oncehuman.game/news/update/20260121/40780_1282991.html) supplies +15% Reload Efficiency for two set pieces. With an entered non-RaidZone scenario and actual base reload seconds, the comparison uses `base_reload_seconds / 1.15` inside its illustrative firing cycle. It does not convert an old Reload Speed display value to seconds.

An optional `context.combat_profile` projects an explicit packet budget through both alternatives. It accepts a shot count, a direct-hit packet with entered crit, weakspot and joint shares, and event packets with a separately entered Attack/Psi basis, Weapon/Status damage class, receiving branch, coefficient, count or chance per shot, repeats, ticks and condition fraction. The projection applies the event's declared Weapon or Status pool independently of its basis and keyword; `elemental_pool_applies` can explicitly override the default branch-based elemental-pool hypothesis. The direct-hit `joint_multiplier` selects additive or multiplicative crit-plus-weakspot math in both baseline and comparison. An entered encounter duration enables conditional damage per second; without duration no DPS is produced. This is an expectation model, not a claim about the game's trigger algorithm or damage bucket order. `combat_projection.reload_seconds(base, efficiency_percent)` implements the [official reload-efficiency transform](https://www.oncehuman.game/news/update/20250521/40780_1235763.html); displayed Reload Speed ratings must first be mapped to actual base seconds. [Official Deviation examples](https://www.oncehuman.game/news/devBlog/20260212/40781_1287008.html) show why damage basis and damage class are separate fields.

The same pure calculation module also provides relative trigger-chance, capped resource coefficient, ordered weighted-counter and periodic-tick primitives. Each requires explicit parameters and rules for carry-over or tick boundaries. They are not automatically bound to a catalog item: [official trigger notes](https://www.oncehuman.game/news/devBlog/20241123/40781_1195665.html) describe a pseudo-random trigger system, so a constant per-shot chance is only a stated expectation model unless the actual weapon rule supports it. Sources, scenario inputs, and unresolved game semantics must travel with a binding before a gear recommendation uses one of these primitives.

All relative damage calculations assume bonuses add inside each named pool, then pools multiply. Their actual in-game stacking and proc applicability remain unverified. Main effects are compared textually and omitted from arithmetic unless their effect is represented in reviewed rows. Missing exact values, unmodeled rows, incomplete catalog profiles, unknown conditions and changed main effects stay in the `missing` list. The API always returns `game_winner_established: false` for this sensitivity calculation. It does not silently infer zero for absent data.

Measured `runs` contain damage, rounds and optional duration, target, buffs, conditions and source. Their totals are reported separately. No duration means no damage-per-second figure; mismatched target, buffs, conditions or round counts are flagged. Even matched runs do not alone establish a causal game winner because sample variability and unrecorded effects remain possible.

The included [helmet example](examples/helmet_lunar_reported.json) is a regression fixture from the conversation's screenshot readings and video review. Its base pool includes the shared Deviation Expert +20% Status DMG core, while other active pools remain unknown. The underlying screenshot files are unavailable in this checkout. The six damage totals were visually checked against the video overlays; gear-to-trial assignment, round counts, buff states and comparable durations were not independently established by that check. On the reported assignment, totals differ by 223 damage across 174 rounds each, with reported buff mismatch and no measured durations. It is not a current-build DPS forecast.

Run `python3 tools/component_coverage.py` for current evidence coverage. The catalog has 985 mod records, of which 984 are selectable variants; their theoretical Lv. 1–17 grid contains 16,728 pairs. The currently resolved grid has **68 complete exact profiles**; the frozen suffix packet lists **75 exact identity-level observations** (68 complete, seven partial). Thirteen newly imported held video/screenshot profiles retain matching source SHA-256 hashes, time marks when applicable, variant and level. Three trigger conditions on Against All Odds <Battle> were corrected from the held video and are never treated as passive damage. Source evidence does not imply ownership or current game-build equivalence. The previously ambiguous monster-damage rows remain unknown. Eight ordinary exact-level main cards and two Shiny Lv. 17 cards have arithmetic operators among 893 catalog identities with effect text. Most main-effect, set, proc, enemy and buff interactions still need sourced rules. The evaluator handles reviewed rows across arbitrary gear, mods and builds, but this coverage is not an all-catalog DPS certification.

`GET /api/evidence-coverage` reports the separate mechanic-binding backlog. `GET /api/evidence-binding/<catalog_id>?level=<level>` returns status, source and unknowns for one catalog item. The Compare panel includes the selected items' binding statuses in its evidence details. The staged packets currently bind **26 of 138 weapons** only partially (112 unbound, zero complete current weapon profiles), **79 set thresholds** (11 official-release, three historical-release, one historical qualitative, one preview and 63 community snapshot candidates), and **38 standalone gear effects** (nine official-release, four historical-release, four historical-preview, 21 unverified). The v3 suffix packet has 2,505 *derived hypotheses*: 938 four-row candidates and 1,567 partial candidates. These are distinct from the 75 source-attributed exact profiles, whose provenance includes held recordings and earlier catalog sources. The derived rows require `suffix_candidate_opt_in: true`, retain unknown rows as null, and show target version, level obtainability and ownership as unverified. Cross-slot evidence corroborates six of eight prior layout candidates and 23 of 50 prior tier candidates; the new packet has 68 scoped corroborated tier rules with no observed conflicts. Candidate math is an explicit scenario, never a current-build verification. The packets are read-only evidence; only separately implemented released rules enter arithmetic. Unknowns remain explicit in each alternative's audit and cannot be interpreted as zero.

Weapon bindings also expose a distinct stat-reference layer: 131 historical catalog records and 112 directly checked third-party database pages, with six typed disagreements preserved (notably SN700's displayed 255 versus 980 damage and 2.8 versus 3.6 reload seconds). Reload scores stay separate from seconds. Neither source is silently treated as the user's crafted tier, calibration, Attack, Psi, or a complete current weapon profile. The 138-row rule map identifies the next missing input for each weapon, including those without a bound keyword mechanic.

A caller may set `community_candidate_opt_in: true` in Compare context to model typed community set modifiers as an **assumption scenario**. For example, `{"community_candidate_opt_in":true,"condition_uptime":{"shield_positive":1}}` shows the candidate Savior 2-piece +10% Weapon and Status values if shielded. Static candidates such as Agent weakspot and Renegade reload are also supported; elemental candidates additionally require a matching `element`. Every such row carries community provenance and an uncertainty warning, and the game winner remains unverified. Official released values such as Bastille +15% Reload Efficiency take precedence and are not double counted. Candidate set effects whose event timing or stat operator is unsupported remain unknown.
